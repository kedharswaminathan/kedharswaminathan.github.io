DFW Hospital Pricing Transparency

Do hospitals' published price files actually let patients compare prices?

How I approached it

Federal rules require hospitals to publish machine-readable files listing what every insurer pays them. I loaded the files from five Dallas–Fort Worth hospitals into DuckDB and focused on one common, shoppable procedure: CPT 73721, an outpatient knee MRI. If price transparency works anywhere, it should work here.

Before any price could be compared, every payer had to be sorted into commercial, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, or something else. The files name payers as free text, so I built an ordered rule cascade and refined it over four versions. Unclassified payer and plan combinations fell from 46% to 1.8%. One catch along the way: plans named "Medicare PFFS" and "Medicare PPO" are private Medicare Advantage plans, and a simple match on "Medicare" would have mixed their rates into the government benchmark.

What I found

Among the four hospitals that publish negotiated dollar rates, the median commercial rate runs from $907 at Medical City Alliance to $1,945 at Baylor, a 2.1× spread, or 3.7× to 8.0× what Medicare pays. Parkland's rate computes to $3,938, but every one of its commercial contracts for this scan is written as a percentage of its own $6,067 list price. Dividing each dollar rate by the list price matched the published percentage on every row. Both kinds of file follow the rule, and nothing in the format tells a reader which is which. Following the rules doesn't make prices comparable.

What this doesn't show

  • The Medicare rate ($243.77) comes from a secondary lookup and hasn't yet been checked against the CMS source file. The hospital-to-hospital comparison, including the 2.1× spread, doesn't depend on it.
  • This covers one procedure. Whether the pattern holds for surgery or childbirth is untested.
  • Five hospitals at one point in time (files pulled in mid-2026) show what the file format allows, not a market-wide average.
  • The two middle hospitals swap places if you use the mean instead of the median. The top and bottom don't move.
